## Study 2: crowded longs in de-gross weeks

| Series, Class D episodes (n 9) | Episode week mean | All weeks | p (lower) | Share negative | Next 4 weeks mean | All | p (upper) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GVIP minus QQQ (primary)** | **-0.97%** | -0.09% | **0.017** | 78% | +0.63% | -0.36% | 0.093 |
| Reconstructed VIP basket minus QQQ | -0.70% | -0.06% | 0.041 | 67% | +0.75% | -0.25% | 0.070 |
| GVIP minus SPY | -0.89% | +0.02% | 0.008 | 100% | +0.43% | +0.06% | 0.31 |
| GVIP beta-residual against QQQ | -0.91% | -0.07% | 0.024 | 89% | +0.72% | -0.31% | 0.093 |

**H-crowd: SUPPORTED.** In the week Goldman reports de-grossing, hedge funds' crowded longs trail
the Nasdaq 100 by about 0.9 percentage points more than in an ordinary week, on the live fund, on the
reconstructed basket and against the S&P 500 alike. Per-episode GVIP minus QQQ, in order: -0.7%,
-1.8%, +0.2%, -3.1%, +0.8%, -1.4%, -0.4%, -1.9%, -0.4%; the result does not rest on one week.

Class N (record net selling, 12 episodes): no such effect against QQQ (+0.28%, p lower 0.87); the
reconstructed basket outperformed QQQ (p upper 0.007). Reported without weight.

**What it does and does not license.**
- It is a coincident relationship: crowded longs fall as funds sell them, the mechanism the crowding
  literature describes. It confirms that the Prime book de-gross weeks are real crowding events.
- It is not a trading signal. The figures cover the week the reports describe, which are published
  after it ends. Over the following four weeks the crowded longs **recovered** more than usual
  (+0.6% against -0.4%; p 0.09, not significant, no verdict weight), so a reader acting on the
  report would, if anything, have sold near the low.
- Nine episodes; the permutation p is exact for the sample but the sample is small.

## Study 4: 13F-copycat ETFs

Retrieval: of six candidates, **four returned no history from Yahoo or Stooq** (GURI, ALFA, ALFI,
IBLN; all apparently delisted). Two were admitted mechanically (GURU, ACTX) and the frozen run
returned FAIL on their composite.

**The frozen verdict is void.** The series returned for `ACTX` cannot be the Global X Guru Activist
ETF: its history starts in January 2010, its annualised volatility is 354%, its maximum drawdown is
-100% and its market beta is negative. The ticker resolves to another security. This is an identity
failure of the input, detectable without reference to alpha, and the PREREG names the fund, not the
ticker. With ACTX removed, one fund is admitted and the PREREG verdict is **INSUFFICIENT**.

The one valid copycat, GURU (Global X Guru Index, 171 months, July 2012 to September 2026):
six-factor alpha **-1.65% a year (t -1.16)**; **-7.1% a year against QQQ**; -2.3% a year against SPY;
maximum drawdown -36.9%; market beta 1.00. It repeats GVIP's pattern. The more telling finding is
the survivorship: four of the six copycat funds no longer exist in either free price source.

## Studies 2a to 2d: the shape of the crowding footprint

Run 29 October 2026, GitHub Actions run id 40921498450 on commit `c2fde72` (run 2 failed on a
module-name collision before computing anything; the fix is in that commit). Pre-registration
`tools/crowd_degross/PREREG_2.md`, committed at `3b0812a` before any figure was computed. Same nine
Class D episodes; Holm adjustment across the four primary tests.

| Study | Primary measure, episode week | Episodes | All weeks / null | p raw | p Holm | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2a | Crowded shorts (B's put-holder basket) minus QQQ | **+1.45%** | -0.09% | 0.028 | 0.083 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| 2b | Momentum factor | **-1.35%** | +0.07% | 0.030 | 0.083 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| 2c | GVIP minus QQQ against rally-matched weeks | **-0.97%** | -0.11% | 0.017 | 0.066 | NOT SUPPORTED |
| 2d | GVIP minus QQQ z <= -2 as a nowcast of the episodes | 1 of 9 hit | chance 9.8% | 0.60 | 0.60 | DOES NOT SEE |

Reported without weight: the crowded long-short spread (VIP basket minus crowded shorts) **-2.15%**
in the episode week against +0.03% (p lower 0.006); shorts give it back over the next four weeks
(-0.35%, level with normal); SMB, HML, RMW and CMA show nothing (two-sided p >= 0.21); in the week
**before** the episode GVIP lags QQQ by 0.69% against 0.09% (p lower 0.064).

**Reading.**
- **By the pre-registered rule, none of the four is supported.** Each of 2a, 2b and 2c is
  significant on its own in the predicted direction, and none survives the Holm adjustment, which
  was fixed in advance and is kept. (Holm is conservative here because the three tests reuse the same
  nine weeks and are positively correlated; that is a reason the rule was written, not a reason to
  set it aside.)
- **The direction is consistent across every measure:** crowded longs fall, crowded shorts rise, the
  long-short spread loses about 2%, momentum falls. That is the footprint the crowding literature
  predicts for a de-grossing, and it is reported as consistent with study 2, not as confirmation of
  it.
- **2c answers the obvious objection.** Against weeks that followed a rally of the same size, the
  crowded longs still lag by about the same amount (-0.97% against -0.11%): study 2's result is not
  produced by de-gross weeks tending to follow rallies.
- **2d closes the practical door.** Only one of nine episode weeks (June 2024) shows up as an extreme
  GVIP-against-QQQ week; most episode weeks are ordinary (z between -1.5 and +0.7). The footprint is
  visible on average across episodes but too small to find a de-gross week from public prices alone.
  The week-before lag (p 0.064) is a hint for a future pre-registered test, not a finding.

The trade's losses are mostly not a signal in reverse: crowded longs lag QQQ by about 0.46% per 20 days

underperformance already recorded in `docs/GVIP_LIVE_2026-10-29.md`.

**Independent implementation:** agrees on every figure. The comparator's one DISAGREE is a basket
verdict the second implementation computed and the first did not (the PREREG requires none).

**Reading.** The four-week recovery after Goldman's de-gross weeks (p 0.09, without weight) does not
generalise to bad weeks for the same basket. The tradeable candidate is closed.
