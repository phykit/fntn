# Rows 32 and 33, and the two non-binding referents re-examined

**27 August 2026. Phase 5 of the nine-phase batch.**

---

## 5a. §13 row 32, novelty roster coverage. **STAYS BLOCKED, and the blocker is now precise.**

**What §3.4 asks for:** *"A first-mention claim from a roster below a stated
coverage threshold is flagged rather than trusted."*

**Coverage is a ratio and it needs both halves:**

```
coverage  =  outlets the system READS  /  outlets that COULD carry a first mention
```

### What is obtainable now, without a purchase and without a design segment

***The numerator is a DECISION, not data, and it is already on the board.***
`docs/DECISION_PACK.md` item 2 lists **source roster** as a **DECISION**: *which
sources are in, at which §3.1 regulatory rank... **No purchase.*** §0.7(g)
already fixes the *rule* — regulatory status, not delivery channel — and what is
missing is the enumerated list. **So half of row 32 is unblocked by a decision
the operator can take this afternoon**, and it is not a new decision: it is
pack item 2, and duplicating it here would put one decision on the board twice.

***The denominator is the blocker, and it CANNOT be taken from inside the
corpus.*** §3.4 states the trap in its own words: *"First-mention share can only
detect prior mentions **inside the corpus the system reads**."* **An outlet
population defined as "the outlets appearing in the archive" makes the roster a
subset of itself, coverage identically 100%, and the threshold vacuous.**
**A vacuous threshold is worse than an unset one**, because it reports a pass.

**So the denominator must be EXTERNAL to the corpus, and there are exactly two
ways to obtain one:**

| Route | Cost | State |
|---|---|---|
| A media-universe list from a vendor | **a purchase**, and §0.7(d)'s ICB vintage is the only other one on the register | not taken |
| A hand-enumerated outlet population for the covered markets | **free, and it is work**; it is the same class of deliverable as §0.7(a)'s documented partition search | not started |

**What remains after the roster decision is taken: the denominator, and nothing
else.** *That is a narrower blocker than "a measurement of roster coverage", and
naming which half is free is the whole of what this phase can do for row 32.*

**Row 32: BLOCKED. Status unchanged.**

## 5b. §13 row 33, §3.6.5's cadence. **PREPARED, DERIVED, and TAKEN in part.**

**What it governs.** §3.6.5 requires collection cadence to be *calendar-driven
and pre-registered*, naming **"a stated day, a stated source page, stated
fields"**, so that *"collection when something interesting happened"* cannot
masquerade as collection. **None of the three was stated.**

### The day. **DERIVED: DAILY, and weekly is refuted by arithmetic.**

**The derivation runs through Gate 1's ingestion-lag check, not through
convenience.**

```
ingestion_lag = t_pub_observed - t_pub_earliest
Gate 1 rejects where the lag exceeds a stated fraction of the admissible horizon
§3.5: "at h = 5 that admits roughly one session"
```

**A weekly cadence puts the mean lag at about 3.5 sessions and the maximum at 7.**
**At h = 5 that fails Gate 1 on nearly every item.**

***So a weekly protocol does not slow the short-horizon family down. It removes
it.*** Every h = 5 tuple would die at `ingestion_lag_exceeds_window`, **silently
and for a reason that is a property of the collection timetable rather than of
the market**, and §0.9's constructive corollary — *"the same arithmetic that
closes long horizons opens short ones"* — would be nullified by a diary.

**Taken: collection runs on EVERY DAY that is a trading day in any covered
market, at a fixed time after the last covered market's close.**

**The time derives too.** ASX closes about 06:00 UTC, the LSE about 16:30 UTC,
the US about 20:00 or 21:00 UTC depending on daylight saving. **A collection
before the last close truncates that market's day systematically**, and a
systematic truncation of one market is exactly the cross-market comparability
§13 row 24 exists to measure. **Fixed at 21:30 UTC**, which is after the latest
of the three in either daylight-saving regime.

**A missed collection is logged as missed** and the next collection uses a
lookback to the previous *successful* one, so **a gap is recorded as a gap and
never as an absence of events**, which is §3.6.5's own wording.

***The cost, stated: this is the most expensive cadence in operator time and it
is taken anyway.*** The cheaper ones are not cheaper; they are narrower, and they
narrow the horizon families rather than the workload.

### The fields. **DERIVED: the fields the intake points read, and no others.**

**Not chosen. A collection that omits a field the observation ladder refuses on
produces items that die at intake for want of something the protocol never
collected**, and the refusal would be attributed to the item rather than to the
diary.

| Field | The intake point that reads it |
|---|---|
| `source_ref`, retrievable | `item_source_inaccessible` |
| `document_type` ∈ {static, running} | `running_document_no_anchor` |
| anchor provenance ∈ {visible_date, feed_timestamp, cms_metadata, inferred} | `anchor_provenance_absent` |
| `t_pub_observed` | `observation_anchor_absent` |
| `t_pub_earliest` | `ingestion_lag_exceeds_window` |
| every mandatory extraction field | `extraction_schema_incomplete` |
| issuer identifier resolvable to a primary listing | `issuer_unresolved` |
| `t_cat_claimed`, and its corroboration | `catalyst_date_corroborated` |
| catalyst duration in sessions | `catalyst_duration_below_floor` |
| source class by §0.7(g) **regulatory status**, never delivery channel | §3.1's rank |

**Ten fields, each named by the point that refuses without it.**

### The source pages. **NOT taken, and the reason is that they are already somebody else's decision.**

***The source pages ARE the source roster.*** Stating them here would take pack
item 2's decision under a different heading, and **a decision taken twice under
two names is a decision nobody can revise once.** They are named as the
remaining part and pointed at item 2.

**Row 33: OPEN → PART CLOSED**, over the day and the fields, with the source
pages outstanding and pointed at pack item 2.

## 5c. The two non-binding referents. **One becomes a row; one does not.**

Each was recorded non-binding with the condition that would make it binding.
**The question is whether that condition is reachable on the current binding
path**, and the two answers differ.

### §5.4.2's surprise-measure fallback ladder. **REACHABLE. §13 row 34 opened.**

The condition was: *the moment the registration names a surprise measure.*

***It is reachable now, and the register already says so without anyone
noticing.*** §13 row 22's registered `discoverable_classes` are
`insider_dealing`, `major_holdings_change`, `buyback` and **`earnings_event`**.
**An earnings-event directive is admissible today.** The moment one is raised
and registered, §5.4.2's drift-direction rule runs, **and it needs a named
surprise measure with a fallback ladder that does not exist.**

**Row 34: BLOCKED**, on the registration naming a surprise measure, and it must
be filled **in the same commit that names one**, because a named measure with no
fallback refuses on its first missing input rather than descending.

### §5.4.3's concert-party band. **NOT reachable. It stays in the sweep record.**

The condition was: *the same commit that takes the Annex A row.*

**That row is deferred behind §0.6's instruments report**, which is downstream of
**step 5**: §7.1 and §7.5 must return a verdict before §0.6 disarms. **So the
condition sits beyond the end of the current binding path, not on it.**

***Opening a §13 row for it would put a row on the register for a parameter of a
capability that cannot be reached until the rule blocking it is disarmed***, and
the register would then carry a blocker that no step can clear. **It stays where
it is**, in `docs/UNDEFINED_REFERENTS_2026-08-27.md` and on the Annex A.1 row's
own predicate, **and it is named here so the distinction between the two is on
the record rather than in a judgement nobody wrote down.**
