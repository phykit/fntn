## 4b does not apply, and the reason is stronger than the mismatch

**Even if the numbers had matched, §14's 200 could not supply what row 21a
lacks.**

Row 21a is blocked for want of a **precision target**. §14's 200 comes from a
**stopping rule**. **Those are different arguments about different quantities,
and one cannot substitute for the other**: a count that says *when tracing has
found enough* says nothing about *how precisely a rate must be known*.

***And an honesty point that cuts against this project's own claim.*** P113
recorded §14's count as **derived**. It is derived **from row 28**, and **row
28's block size of 100 was argued from feasibility**, not from a power
calculation: two blocks of 100 is about three times all the tracing ever done
here, and doubling it costs 400 more items. **So neither 200 rests on a power
calculation.** *Row 21a is blocked precisely for want of one, and §14's 200 does
not have one to lend.*

## What this phase adds without unblocking anything

***The blocker can be stated as an equation, and that is worth more than
stating it as an absence.*** Row 21a's own rule of three inverts:

```
zero events in n trials  ->  95% upper bound = 3/n
so, for a target upper bound b,   n = 3/b
```

| Target upper bound `b` | Required `n`, labelled drawn proposals |
|---|---|
| 0.5% | 600 |
| 1% | 300 |
| 2% | 150 |
| 5% | 60 |
| **8.3%** | **36** — *which is where the row stands today* |

**So row 21a is blocked on exactly ONE number, `b`, and not on a sample-size
question at all.** Once §7.1 states how much funnel depth it can afford to lose,
**`n` follows by arithmetic and no decision is left.**

*That is a narrower and better-shaped blocker than "no sample size can be
derived", and it is the whole of what this phase changes.* **The row's status,
reading and blocker are untouched.**

*The withdrawn 200 corresponds to `b` = 1.5%, which nobody ever stated.*
